How to Choose Between Petrol, Diesel, CNG, and Electric

Selecting a fuel type isn't just about the fuel price; it's about the Total Cost of Ownership (TCO). Petrol cars are usually cheaper to buy but costlier to run. CNG and Diesel cars have a "premium" purchase price but offer significantly lower running costs. Electric Vehicles (EVs) have the highest upfront cost but the lowest per-km running expense. This calculator helps you find the "breakeven point" where the fuel savings finally cover the extra cost you paid for the car.

A Practical Comparison Example

Scenario A: Petrol Car

  • Car Price: ₹8,00,000
  • Mileage: 15 kmpl
  • Monthly Running: 1,000 km
  • 5-Year Running Cost: Approx ₹4,20,000

Scenario B: Electric Vehicle (EV)

  • Car Price: ₹12,00,000
  • Efficiency: 7 km/kWh
  • Monthly Running: 1,000 km
  • 5-Year Charging Cost: Approx ₹68,500

Verdict: The EV saves ₹3.5 Lakhs in fuel over 5 years. If the purchase gap is ₹4 Lakhs, you'll need nearly 6 years to break even on fuel savings alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is CNG always cheaper?

Only if your running is high. If you drive less than 500km a month, it may take 7-10 years to recover the extra purchase cost of a CNG kit.

When does an EV make sense?

EVs are ideal for city commutes with daily running above 40-50km. They offer a silent drive and the lowest running costs (often under ₹1.5 per km), provided you have home charging facilities.

Why consider Diesel?

Diesel offers the best torque for highway driving and better long-distance fuel efficiency, though it has the highest maintenance costs and stricter environmental regulations in certain zones.

How to Use the Fuel Cost Comparison Calculator

Follow these step-by-step instructions to accurately compare the long-term costs of your next vehicle:

  • 1. Select Vehicle Types: Use the checkboxes at the top of the input panel to toggle Petrol, Diesel, CNG, or Electric (EV) options. This allows you to compare two or all four types side-by-side.
  • 2. Enter Purchase Price: Input the "On-Road" price for each vehicle variant. Note that EVs and Diesel cars typically have a higher upfront cost than Petrol.
  • 3. Mileage / Efficiency: Enter the expected real-world mileage. For Petrol/Diesel, use km per litre (kmpl). For CNG, use km per kg. For Electric Vehicles, enter the battery efficiency in km per kWh (usually between 6-9 km/unit).
  • 4. Fuel or Electricity Cost: Provide the current price of fuel in your city. For EVs, enter your local domestic or commercial electricity slab rate per kWh.
  • 5. Monthly Running: Adjust the slider or input your average monthly distance in kilometers. Higher usage typically favors CNG and EV options.
  • 6. Ownership Duration: Select the number of years you plan to keep the vehicle. The calculator will estimate total costs for this specific time horizon.
  • 7. Advanced Conditions: Enable the "Include Advanced Costs" toggle to add annual maintenance expenses, estimated resale value percentages, and even planned battery replacement costs for Electric Vehicles.
  • 8. Understanding Results: The right-side panel displays ownership cards. The most economical option is highlighted in green, while the most expensive is in red. The summary banner provides a clear savings breakdown between your best and worst options.

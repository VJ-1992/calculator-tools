Discount Calculation Formulas

The math behind standard discounts is straightforward:

Discount Amount = (Original Price × Discount %) / 100

Final Price = Original Price - Discount Amount

Sequential Discount Logic

For sequential or stackable discounts, the second discount is applied to the balance *after* the first discount. For instance, if you have a 20% discount followed by an extra 10%, the final price is 72% of the original, not 70%.

Types of Discounts Explained

Percentage Discount

The most common type where the price is reduced by a certain portion (e.g., 25% off).

Flat Amount Discount

A fixed monetary value taken off the price (e.g., Save ₹200 on your purchase).

Sequential Discounts

Multiple discounts applied one after the other, often used in "stackable" retail promotions.

Discount with GST

Calculating the final subtotal after rebates and then adding the applicable sales tax.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is a discount?

A discount is a reduction in the regular price of a product or service, typically used in sales promotions or bulk purchasing.

How is discount percentage calculated?

Divide the discount amount by the original price and multiply by 100 to find the percentage decrease.

What is the difference between flat and percentage discount?

Flat discounts take a fixed amount off (e.g., -₹100), while percentage discounts scale with the item's price (e.g., 10% off).

How do multiple discounts work?

They are usually applied one after another. The first discount reduces the original price, and the next discount is calculated based on that new subtotal.

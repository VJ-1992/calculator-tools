How to Use the Profit Margin Calculator

Understanding your numbers is key to business growth. Use this tool to quickly find out how much you are actually making on your products:

  • Cost Price: Enter the total cost to produce or purchase the item, including shipping and manufacturing.
  • Selling Price: Enter the price you are charging the customer.
  • Results: The calculator instantly shows your Net Profit (the dollar amount) and your Gross Margin (the percentage).

Formula Explanation

Profit margin is a ratio of a company's profit divided by its revenue. Here is the math we use:

Net Profit = Selling Price - Cost Price
Gross Margin (%) = (Net Profit / Selling Price) × 100

It is important to distinguish this from Markup. While markup is calculated based on the cost, margin is always calculated based on the selling price. Margin tells you what percentage of every dollar you keep as profit.

Example Calculation

Let's say you buy a product for $60 and sell it for $100.

  1. Cost Price = $60
  2. Selling Price = $100
  3. Net Profit = $100 - $60 = $40.
  4. Gross Margin = ($40 / $100) × 100 = 40%.

In this scenario, for every $1 you receive in sales, you keep $0.40 as gross profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a "good" profit margin?

Good margins vary by industry. Retail often averages between 20% and 40%, while SaaS (software) companies can have margins as high as 80%. Consult with a financial advisor to understand benchmarks for your specific niche.

Is Gross Margin the same as Net Margin?

No. Gross margin only considers the "cost of goods sold" (COGS). Net margin subtracts all other business expenses like rent, marketing, salaries, and taxes.

What if my margin is negative?

A negative margin means your cost price is higher than your selling price. This indicates you are losing money on every sale and should re-evaluate your pricing strategy or supply costs.

Does this tool include shipping costs?

Yes, as long as you include those costs in your "Cost Price" input. For the most accurate result, ensure you are using the "Landed Cost" (total cost to get the product to the customer).

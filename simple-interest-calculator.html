How to Use the Simple Interest Calculator

Our tool helps you determine the growth of an investment or the cost of a loan over a specific period. Follow these simple steps:

  • Principal Amount: Enter the initial amount of money borrowed or invested.
  • Interest Rate: Enter the annual interest rate as a percentage (e.g., 5.5).
  • Time Period: Enter the duration. You can toggle between Years or Months to match your terms.
  • Comparison: Optionally check the "Compare with Compound Interest" box to see how much more you would earn/owe if interest was compounded annually.

Formula Explanation

Simple interest is calculated only on the principal amount of a loan or investment. The formula used by this calculator is:

Interest = Principal (P) × Rate (R) × Time (T) / 100
Maturity Value = Principal + Interest

Note that "Time" must be expressed in years. If you enter months, the tool automatically divides by 12 to convert it to the yearly equivalent required for the standard rate.

Example Calculation

Let's say you invest $1,000 at an annual simple interest rate of 5% for 3 years.

  1. Principal (P) = $1,000
  2. Rate (R) = 5%
  3. Time (T) = 3 years
  4. Interest = (1,000 × 5 × 3) / 100 = $150.
  5. The total maturity value after 3 years will be $1,150.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Simple and Compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated solely on the principal amount. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus any interest that has already accumulated, leading to faster growth over time.

Can I calculate interest for just a few months?

Yes. Simply use the toggle to select "Months" and enter the number of months. The calculator will handle the conversion automatically.

Does this calculator work for car loans?

Many personal and car loans use compound interest or specific amortized formulas. However, this tool is excellent for estimating basic debt growth or checking private loan agreements that specify "simple interest" terms.

Is the interest rate per month or per year?

Standard financial practice assumes the rate is per annum (p.a.), meaning per year. Our calculator follows this standard.

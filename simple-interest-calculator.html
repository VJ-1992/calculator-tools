Difference Between Simple and Compound Interest

Choosing the right calculation mode depends on how your bank or investment firm processes returns. Here is a breakdown of both:

Simple Interest (SI)

"Interest on the original sum only."

Simple interest is calculated solely on the principal amount. It's often used for short-term personal loans or specific bond types.

Compound Interest (CI)

"Interest on interest."

Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus the accumulated interest from previous periods. This leads to exponential growth over time.

The Mathematical Formulas

Simple Interest Formula

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Compound Interest Formula

A = P (1 + r/n)nt

P = Principal

R = Annual Rate (%)

T = Time (Years)

N = Compounding Freq

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for long-term?

For investments, Compound Interest is vastly superior due to the effect of "interest on interest." For loans, Simple Interest is generally cheaper for the borrower.

What is compounding frequency?

It is the number of times interest is added to the principal per year. The more frequent the compounding (e.g., monthly vs. yearly), the higher the final amount.
